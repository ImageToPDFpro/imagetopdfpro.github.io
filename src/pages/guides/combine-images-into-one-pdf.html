Sending ten separate photos is messy: the recipient has to open each one, and there is no guarantee they'll view them in the right order. Combining the images into a single PDF solves that. Everything travels as one file, the pages stay in sequence, and it prints properly. Here's how to do it well.

When a single PDF is the right choice

  • Multi-page documents photographed page by page, such as contracts, forms, bank statements or certificates.
  • Front and back of ID cards, driving licences or payment cards for verification.
  • Receipts and invoices for an expense claim or tax return.
  • Assignments and handwritten homework that must be submitted as one file.
  • Portfolios of artwork, designs or product photos.

Step by step: merge images into one PDF

  1. Open the converter and add all your images at once. You can select many files in the file picker by holding Ctrl (Windows) or ⌘ (Mac), or by dragging a selection from your file manager. Mixed formats are fine: JPG, PNG, HEIC and WebP can go in the same PDF.
  2. Need more? Click Add images or the Add more tile at any time. New images are added to the end.
  3. Set the order. Drag the cards into position, or use the arrow buttons below each image. Numbers in the corner show the final page order.
  4. Fix orientation. Rotate any sideways or upside-down image in 90° steps.
  5. Keep Output on One PDF, then choose page size, margin and quality.
  6. Give it a clear name, such as passport-application-2026, then click Convert to PDF and download.

Getting the page order right

For a handful of images, dragging is quickest. For larger batches, let the file names or dates do the work:

Sort optionUse it when…
Name (A to Z)Your files are numbered, like page-01.jpg, page-02.jpg. Numbers are sorted naturally, so page 10 comes after page 9.
Oldest firstYou photographed the pages in order. Photos are sorted by their last-modified date.
Newest firstYou shot the pages backwards, from the last page to the first.
Reverse orderEverything is in the right sequence but backwards.

Tip: When photographing a multi-page document, take a picture of the first page last as a "marker", or simply keep going in order without deleting photos in between. Sorting by date then gives you a perfect sequence.

Choosing a consistent layout

Images from different sources often have different shapes: a tall phone photo, a wide screenshot, a square scan. Here's how the settings keep your PDF tidy:

  • Page size A4 or Letter gives every page the same paper size, which is best for printing and official documents.
  • Orientation: Auto turns each page landscape for wide images and portrait for tall ones. Choose Portrait if every page must be upright.
  • Image fit: Fit whole image never cuts anything off. Fill page removes the white space but may trim edges.
  • Page size: Same as image makes every page exactly the shape of its picture. Great for screenshots and artwork, less so for printing.

One PDF or separate PDFs?

Sometimes a portal asks for each document as its own file, for example one PDF for your ID and another for proof of address. Set Output to PDF per image and the converter creates a separate PDF for every image, packaged in a single ZIP download. Files are numbered in your chosen order.

How many images can I combine?

There is no artificial limit. Because the PDF is built on your device, the practical limit is your device's memory:

  • Laptops and desktops can comfortably combine hundreds of full-resolution photos.
  • Modern phones handle 50–100 photos at Original quality without trouble.
  • Older phones or huge batches: choose Balanced quality. It uses far less memory and creates a much smaller file.

Frequently asked questions

Can I add more pages to a PDF I already made?

The converter creates new PDFs from images. If you still have the tab open, press Back to editing, add the extra images and convert again.

Will the images be resized to match each other?

Each image is scaled to fit its page, so all pages look consistent. The original pixels aren't changed unless you choose a compression preset.

Can I combine images and existing PDF files?

Not currently. The converter accepts images only. You can take screenshots of PDF pages or export them as images first.
