If you have tried to upload iPhone photos to a website and got an error, the culprit is usually HEIC. Since iOS 11, iPhones save photos in this format by default. It keeps photos small and sharp, but many websites and Windows apps still don't accept it. The good news: turning HEIC photos into a PDF is easy once you know where to look.

What is HEIC?

HEIC (High Efficiency Image Container) is Apple's name for the HEIF image format. It stores a photo in roughly half the space of a JPG of similar quality, which is why Apple uses it. The downside is compatibility: browsers such as Chrome and Firefox on Windows and Android can't display HEIC files, and many upload forms reject them.

When you convert HEIC photos into a PDF, compatibility stops being a problem because every device can open a PDF.

Method 1: The free online converter (fastest for many photos)

Our image to PDF converter accepts HEIC and HEIF files directly. In browsers that can't decode HEIC, it converts the photos on your device automatically. Nothing is uploaded.

  1. On your iPhone, open Safari (or any browser) and go to the converter.
  2. Tap Choose images, then Photo Library, and select your photos. You can also take a new picture with the camera.
  3. Arrange the pages. On a touch screen, use the handle in the corner of each card to drag it.
  4. Tap Convert to PDF, then Share to save it to Files, send it by Mail or WhatsApp, or Download PDF to save it to your Downloads folder.

On a computer? If you have copied HEIC files from your iPhone to a Windows PC or Chromebook, just drag them into the converter. It handles the HEIC decoding for you, which usually takes a second or two per photo.

Method 2: The Files app "Create PDF" action

iOS and iPadOS include a built-in way to create a PDF from images stored in the Files app.

  1. Open Photos, select your pictures, tap the Share button and choose Save to Files. Pick a folder, such as On My iPhone.
  2. Open the Files app and go to that folder.
  3. Tap the More button (three dots) and choose Select, then tap the images you want in the PDF.
  4. Tap the More button at the bottom right and choose Create PDF.

A new PDF appears in the same folder. This method is convenient, but you have limited control: pages follow the order of the files in the folder, and you can't choose paper size or compression.

Method 3: The Print-to-PDF trick

This older trick works from almost any app that can print.

  1. In Photos, select the images and tap Share → Print.
  2. In the print preview, pinch outwards on the preview with two fingers. This opens the document as a PDF.
  3. Tap Share again and choose Save to Files or send it to another app.

The Print trick gives you a PDF sized for printer paper, typically with white margins around each photo.

Stop HEIC problems in the future

If you frequently upload photos to websites, you can make your iPhone save new photos as JPG instead:

  1. Open Settings → Camera → Formats.
  2. Choose Most Compatible.

Photos will take up more storage, but they will work everywhere. You can also leave HEIC on and let your iPhone convert automatically when transferring to a computer: go to Settings → Photos and, under Transfer to Mac or PC, choose Automatic.

Tips for document photos on iPhone

  • Use the Notes app scanner for paperwork: open a note, tap the camera icon and choose Scan Documents. It straightens pages automatically.
  • Turn off Live Photos for documents to avoid blur from movement.
  • Tap to focus on the text before taking the photo, and avoid using the flash on glossy paper.

Frequently asked questions

Does converting HEIC to PDF lose quality?

Barely. HEIC must be decoded because PDF can't store HEIC data directly, so the photo is saved as a high-quality JPEG inside the PDF. At the Original quality setting the difference is invisible.

Why can't I see my HEIC photo on Windows?

Windows needs the HEIF and HEVC extensions from the Microsoft Store to preview HEIC files. You don't need them to use our converter.

Can I convert Live Photos?

Yes. When you pick a Live Photo, iOS provides the still image, which is what goes into the PDF.
