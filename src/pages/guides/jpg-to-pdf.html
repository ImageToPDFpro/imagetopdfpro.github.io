JPG (also written JPEG) is the most common image format in the world. Every phone camera, scanner app and screenshot tool can produce it. But when a website, school or employer asks you for a PDF, a folder of JPG files won't do. This guide shows you how to convert JPG to PDF in under a minute, on any device, while keeping your pictures sharp.

JPG vs PDF: what's the difference?

A JPG is a single picture. It is great for photos but has no concept of pages, paper size or page order. A PDF (Portable Document Format) is a document container. It can hold many pages, each with a defined size such as A4 or US Letter, and it looks the same on every device and when printed.

Converting your JPGs to PDF doesn't have to change the image itself. A good converter simply places each JPG onto a page, which is exactly what our converter does with the Original quality setting.

Method 1: Use the free online converter (all devices)

This method works the same on Windows, macOS, ChromeOS, Linux, Android and iPhone, and your photos are never uploaded because the conversion runs inside your browser.

  1. Open the Image to PDF converter in any modern browser.
  2. Add your JPG files. Drag them onto the page, click Choose images, or paste an image you copied with Ctrl + V (⌘ + V on a Mac). On a phone, tap Choose images and pick photos from your gallery.
  3. Put the pages in order. Drag the cards around, or use the Sort menu to order them by file name or date. Rotate any sideways photos with the rotate button.
  4. Choose your settings. A4 is standard in most countries; choose US Letter in the United States and Canada. Leave quality on Original unless you need a smaller file.
  5. Press Convert to PDF and then Download PDF. On mobile you can also tap Share to send it straight to WhatsApp, email or Google Drive.

Tip: Name your files with numbers, such as 01-front.jpg and 02-back.jpg, and then use Sort → Name (A to Z). The pages will be in the right order instantly, even for dozens of images.

Method 2: Built-in tools on Windows and Mac

Both major desktop operating systems can create a PDF from images without extra software, though with fewer options.

Windows 10 and 11

  1. Select your JPG files in File Explorer.
  2. Right-click and choose Print (on Windows 11, you may need Show more options first).
  3. Choose Microsoft Print to PDF as the printer and pick a layout.
  4. Untick Fit picture to frame if you don't want the edges cropped, then click Print and choose where to save.

macOS

  1. Select the images in Finder.
  2. Right-click and choose Quick Actions → Create PDF.

We cover these methods in more detail in our guide to converting images to PDF on Windows and Mac.

Method 3: On your phone

On an iPhone, open the Files app, select your images, tap the More button (three dots) and choose Create PDF. Photos stored in the Photos app need to be saved to Files first. Our iPhone guide explains this step by step.

On Android, most gallery apps offer Print → Save as PDF, but each app looks different and you can usually only print one photo at a time. The online converter is faster when you have several pages.

Will converting JPG to PDF reduce quality?

It depends on the tool. Many converters decode your JPG and compress it again, which loses detail every time. Our converter copies JPG data into the PDF without re-compressing when quality is set to Original, so the images inside the PDF are bit-for-bit identical to your files.

If you pick High, Balanced or Small file, images are resized and recompressed to make the PDF smaller. For most documents, Balanced is visually indistinguishable on screen. See how to reduce PDF file size for help choosing.

Common problems and fixes

A photo appears sideways

Phone cameras store rotation information separately from the image. Most apps read it, but not all. Just press the rotate button on that card until the preview is upright. The PDF matches the preview.

There are white borders around my photo

The photo's shape doesn't match the page shape. Choose Image fit → Fill page to remove the borders (edges may be trimmed), set Margin → None, or pick Page size → Same as image so each page is exactly the size of its photo.

The PDF is too big to upload

Modern phone photos are often 3–6 MB each. Switch quality to Balanced or Small file and convert again. The file usually shrinks by 70–90%.

Frequently asked questions

Can I combine multiple JPGs into one PDF?

Yes. Add all of them at once and keep Output set to One PDF. Each image becomes a page. Read more in how to combine images into one PDF.

Is JPEG different from JPG?

No. They are the same format. The three-letter .jpg extension dates back to older versions of Windows that limited extensions to three characters.

Can I convert JPG to PDF offline?

Yes. Once you have opened the converter once, it is cached by your browser and continues to work without an internet connection.
