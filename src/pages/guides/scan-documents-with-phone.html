You don't need a flatbed scanner to create professional-looking PDFs. The camera in your pocket is more than sharp enough. What separates a crisp, easy-to-read "scan" from a dim, tilted photo is mostly technique. This guide covers everything from lighting to the final PDF.

Before you start: set up the shot

Find good, even light

Lighting matters more than anything else. Natural daylight near a window is ideal. Avoid direct sunlight, which creates harsh contrast, and avoid a single overhead lamp, which casts the shadow of your phone and hand across the page.

  • Turn off the flash for documents. It creates a bright hotspot and glare, especially on glossy paper.
  • Watch your shadow. If you see the shadow of your phone, move so the light comes from the side or in front of you.

Use a contrasting background

Place white paper on a dark table, or a dark document on a light surface. Contrast helps your eye and scanning apps find the edges of the page, and makes cropping easier.

Flatten the page

Unfold creases and weigh down curled corners with a few coins placed outside the page area. For books, press the spine gently and photograph one page at a time.

Taking the photo

  1. Hold the phone parallel to the page. If the camera is tilted, the page looks like a trapezoid and text on one side becomes smaller. Many camera apps show a level indicator when you look straight down.
  2. Fill the frame with the document, leaving a small border so no edge is cut off.
  3. Don't use digital zoom. Move the phone closer instead; zooming reduces detail.
  4. Tap to focus on the text, and wait a moment for the camera to adjust.
  5. Hold steady. Brace your elbows on the table, or take two shots and keep the sharper one.
  6. Check the result by zooming in on the smallest text before moving to the next page.

Use your phone's built-in scanner

Scanner features automatically detect page edges, straighten perspective and boost contrast, so text looks like a real scan. You can then combine the results into one PDF with exactly the page size you need.

iPhone and iPad

  1. Open the Notes app and create a new note, or open the Files app.
  2. In Notes, tap the camera or paperclip icon and choose Scan Documents. In Files, tap the More button and choose Scan Documents.
  3. Hold the iPhone over the page. It captures automatically when the page is detected, or tap the shutter button.
  4. Drag the corners to adjust the crop if needed, then tap Keep Scan. Repeat for each page and tap Save.

Android

  1. Open the Google Drive app and tap the Scan (camera) button.
  2. Take a photo of the page. Drive crops and enhances it automatically; you can adjust the crop and apply filters.
  3. Tap + to add more pages, then save.

Many Android camera apps, including those on Samsung Galaxy phones, can also detect documents automatically and offer to scan them when you point the camera at a page.

Special cases

ID cards, passports and licences

  • Photograph the front and back separately, then combine them into one PDF with Margin: Large and Fit whole image, so the whole card is always visible.
  • Laminated cards reflect light. Tilt the card or the light source slightly until the glare moves off the text and photo.
  • Never crop away the edges of an ID. Many verification checks require all four corners.
  • Use High quality: IDs have tiny print and security details.

Receipts

Thermal-paper receipts fade over time, so scan them soon. They are long and narrow, which is where Page size: Same as image shines, or keep A4 with Fit whole image for expense systems that expect standard pages.

Multi-page documents

Photograph pages in order without deleting shots in between, then use Sort → Oldest first in the converter. Read more in how to combine images into one PDF.

Whiteboards and handwritten notes

Shoot straight on, as close as possible, and avoid reflections from ceiling lights. A scanner app's "whiteboard" or "black and white" filter makes marker and pencil much easier to read.

Final checklist before you send

  • Every page is present, in the right order, and upright.
  • No edges are cut off and there are no fingers in the shot.
  • The smallest text is readable when zoomed in.
  • The file size is within the upload limit. If not, see how to reduce PDF file size.
  • The file has a clear name, such as lastname-bank-statement-june.pdf.

A word on privacy

Scans of IDs, bank statements and medical records are exactly the kind of files you don't want sitting on a stranger's server. Our converter builds the PDF on your phone, without uploading anything. When you share the PDF, use a trusted channel such as the official portal or a secure email service, and delete old copies you no longer need.

Frequently asked questions

Is a phone scan as good as a real scanner?

For most documents, yes. A modern phone camera easily captures 300 DPI on an A4 page. Flatbed scanners still win for very fine detail, fragile originals and perfectly even lighting.

Should I scan in colour or black and white?

Use colour for IDs, stamps, signatures in blue ink and anything with photos. Black and white is fine for plain text and produces smaller files.

Why are my scans blurry?

Blur usually comes from low light (the camera uses a slower shutter) or moving while shooting. Add light, brace your arms, and tap to focus before capturing.
